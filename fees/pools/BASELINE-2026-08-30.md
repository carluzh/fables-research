### NVDA's "OPEN: hold" was not achievable as written

The routine opening bell is not a stored field. `SessionLib.sol:118` computes it as
`overnightFloor * spikeMult`. Live that is `800 x 5 = 4000`, deliberately set equal to `closedSpike`.

Moving overnight to 550 would therefore have done two things nobody asked for, both silently, both
passing `setPoolConfig` validation:

- the routine bell drops **4000 to 2750**, a 31% cut to the OPEN tier described as untouched, and the
  only tier on any Fables pool earning above its field at 1.19x APR;
- `closedSpike` is absolute and stays at 4000, so **Monday opens become dearer than Tuesday opens**,
  inverting an equality that was set on purpose.

Holding the bell near 4000 with overnight at 550 needs `spikeMult` 7 (3,850) or 8 (4,400), with
`closedSpike` restated to match. No integer lands on 4000. That is a decision, not a derivation.

### GLD's closed floor has no owner

The deviation spec dropped its "revert closed to 1,500" instruction because 1,500 sits below
`pokeFloor` of 3,000. This document says "no change" because GLD is mid-dislocation. Between the two,
**nothing reverts the emergency 6,000**, so it persists by default rather than by decision. It needs
one call: keep 6,000, or pick a considered floor at or above 3,000.

## 4. The elasticity, corrected, and it is weaker than it looked

The afternoon version was produced by a bucketing bug: `int(round(pips/100)*100)` applied to a fee
derived as `1e6*f/v` sends a 250-pip tier to either the 200 or the 300 bucket depending on floating
point noise, so single tiers appeared as two rows and were compared against each other. Rebucketed
on the realised fee rounded to 1 pip, with **measured dollars per hour beside the inferred index**,
because the two disagree in sign.

| asset | pips | hrs | days | session | share | **$/hour** | index |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| SPY | 250 | 38 | 3 | CLOSED | 12.18% | **$21.81** | 3,046 |
| SPY | 350 | 77 | 5 | OVERNIGHT | 9.15% | $6.43 | 3,203 |
| SPY | 500 | 24 | 4 | OPEN | 4.94% | $6.01 | 2,468 |
| SPY | 800 | 6 | **1** | OPEN | 1.85% | **$9.65** | 1,479 |
| NVDA | 300 | 55 | 4 | CLOSED | 0.27% | $0.47 | 82 |
| NVDA | 400 | 64 | 4 | OVERNIGHT | 0.22% | $1.45 | 87 |
| NVDA | 700 | 12 | 4 | OPEN | 0.13% | $1.50 | 93 |
| META | 250 | 37 | 3 | CLOSED | 21.14% | $0.80 | 5,285 |
| META | 350 | 57 | 5 | OVERNIGHT | 21.12% | $0.29 | 7,392 |
| META | 500 | 22 | 4 | OPEN | 21.48% | $0.94 | 10,740 |
| META | 900 | 6 | **1** | OPEN | **41.33%** | $2.68 | 37,194 |
| **GLD** | **300** | **23** | **2** | **CLOSED** | **31.32%** | **$62.47** | 9,397 |
| **GLD** | **6,000** | **14** | **2** | **CLOSED** | **12.60%** | **$330.37** | 75,600 |

Three things this changes.

**META's elasticity is incoherent and cannot support a raise.** Share at 900 pips is **41.33%**,
higher than the 21.14% at 250. A demand curve does not slope up. That is the session confound: the
900-pip hours are six hours of one Friday. META's whole top-of-curve evidence is $2.68 an hour on one
day. The raise is cut to a step whose only justification is the *level* gap, which is measured:
0.08x of market fee is a fact, not an inference.

**SPY's "revenue peaks at 450" was an index artefact.** On measured dollars the 800-pip session hours
earned **$9.65/h against $6.01/h at 500**. The index ranked them the other way. Both readings are
thin (6 hours on one day against 24 hours on four), so the honest statement is that SPY's session
optimum is unknown, not that it is 450.

**GLD is the only real experiment in the set, and it is the only clean one.** 300 and 6,000 pips,
both in CLOSED hours, both across two days: a 20x raise cut share from 31.32% to 12.60% (0.40x) and
multiplied measured dollars per hour **5.29x**, $62.47 to $330.37. Note the correct multiplier is
5.29x per hour, not the 9.3x index ratio quoted this afternoon.

**Every top-of-curve observation on SPY, META and NVDA falls on Friday 2026-08-28**, which is also
the only cash session in the 48h chain scan. The two windows are therefore **not independent
evidence** where it matters most.

## 5. Structural facts that survive any fee change

Corrected where the review found them overstated.

- **We keep 100% of what we charge.** So do **21 of the 50 pools** read, not just us. Against the
  rivals that *do* take a cut the advantage is 14% to 33% relative. The v4 protocol fee is an
  absolute pip amount, not a proportion, so a rival's keep rate moves with its own charged fee.
- **Half the SPY market is unreachable.** 49.1% of chain SPY volume over 48h routed through
  v3 WETH/SPY, 52.1% in closed hours. We do not quote that pair.
- **Concentration, on ONE basis** (window-median virtual over TVL; the afternoon version mixed a
  point reading for SPY with medians elsewhere): SPY **34.2, rank 7 of 8**; NVDA 22.6, rank 9 of 12
  with three rivals shallower; META 33.2, rank 3 of 5; GLD 38.7, rank 1 of 5. SPY is near the bottom
  of its field, not mid-field, and "our NVDA dollar quotes half the depth anyone else's does" was
  false: it compared against three hand-picked venues.
- **Near-free venues exist and must stay in the benchmark.** NVDA has one realising 9 pips on $1.68M;
  ETH has one at 0 pips on $3.5M.
- **Some venues look alive weekly and are dead daily.** Two WETH/NVDA v4 pools showed $7.9M and
  $1.5M over 7 days and zero swaps in 48 hours, and they were inflating the NVDA market fee by 43%.
